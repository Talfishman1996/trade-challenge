## Real Product Job

TradeVault is not primarily an analytics dashboard. Its repeated job is to preserve
one decision cycle across interruptions and devices:

1. **Act:** know the allowed next risk and record the realized result.
2. **Confirm:** know that the trade is locally durable and whether cloud continuity
   is pending, complete, offline, conflicted, or failed.
3. **Understand:** see exactly what changed in equity, progress, streak, drawdown,
   and next risk.
4. **Review:** find the record later, correct it, and extract patterns.

The primary object is therefore a trustworthy trade record and its state transition,
not a chart, projection, monument, or isolated metric card. Those can support the
loop but cannot replace it.

## Job Priority

| Tier | Jobs | Required frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | orient, inspect risk, record result, confirm durability, understand change | every trade / device switch |
| Secondary | review history, find/edit/delete/recover, inspect performance/drawdown | weekly or after notable outcomes |
| Administrative | settings, import/export, sync history | infrequent |
| Recovery | offline log, reconnect, conflict, storage/network failure | rare but trust-defining |

- A minimum loss takes four direct interactions because `LOSS` must be selected.

  with no instrument identifier.
- Reviewing the saved record requires one destination change to History.

## Foreground Sync Coupling

The local state transition and the sheet-dismiss transition are different events in
source but are presented as one blocking operation:

- `useTrades.persist` writes browser storage and updates React state immediately,
  then starts `pushToCloud` without awaiting it.
- `App.handleTradeSave` immediately starts a second sync path and awaits it.
- `TradeEntry.commitTrade` awaits `onSave` and closes only after that promise settles.

Under a deterministic 2.5-second delay per intercepted Worker request:

| Checkpoint | Equity updated | Sheet open | Save locked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 ms | yes | yes | yes |
| 2,600 ms | yes | yes | yes |
| 5,300 ms | yes | no | no |

Two requests were added by the save. The full sample completed in 5,783 ms. This is
not network slowness masquerading as local-save slowness: local equity had already
changed at 100 ms. The workflow waits for cloud work after the accepted local action.

This strengthens existing `F-SYNC-HIGH-005` rather than creating an overlapping
sync finding.

## Outcome-Default Risk

The form initializes new records as Win and Long. A positive amount can be saved
without explicitly confirming either value. A user logging a loss must notice and
change the default; failure to do so records the opposite outcome and changes all
downstream equity/model/analytics state. This opens `F-UX-HIGH-001`.

## Product Implications

- The future primary flow should commit locally, close or advance immediately, and
  expose cloud progress independently without blocking the user's next action.
- Outcome must be explicit or derivable from a signed amount; a correctness-critical
  value should not silently inherit a positive default.
- Post-save feedback should state what changed and provide a direct review/edit
  affordance without requiring the user to infer success from several moving cards.
- Recovery states belong inside the primary loop because they determine whether the
  record can be trusted across devices.

## Limits

These are controlled-browser measurements, not human completion-time data. Tap
counts and state transitions are valid; automated elapsed time does not establish
human speed. Physical keyboard, background/resume, and device-switch behavior remain
owned by P3.3, P6, and P8.
